Quang Trung (Chinese characters: 光中, 1753 – September 16, 1792), whose pre-coronation title was Bac Binh Vuong (北平王 - Prince of Northern Peace), birth name Ho Thom (胡𦹳), originally from Nghe An and later renamed Nguyen Hue (阮惠), was a Vietnamese politician and military leader. He was the 2nd emperor of the Tay Son Dynasty after his older brother, Emperor Thai Duc Nguyen Nhac, abdicated and passed the throne to him.

Nguyen Hue and his two brothers, known as the Tay Son heroes (Tây Sơn tam kiệt), were the leaders of the Tay Son uprising that ended the Trinh–Nguyen civil war between the two feudal groups of Trinh in the north and Nguyen in the south, overthrowing these two groups along with the Later Le Dynasty, ending the division of Dang Trong (Inner Realm) and Dang Ngoai (Outer Realm) that lasted for 2 centuries. In addition, Quang Trung was also the one who defeated the invasions of Dai Viet by Siam from the south and the Great Qing from the north. He personally commanded troops from the age of 18, and over 20 consecutive years, he fought in dozens of major battles without losing a single one.

Historian Phan Huy Le evaluated that "Quang Trung was not only a brilliant military leader but also a politician with exceptional talent"[1]. With a progressive vision, in just 3 years, he successively proposed many progressive reform plans in economics, culture, education, military,... to build the country and absorb modern science and technology from the West.[2] Regarding personnel, he issued an edict seeking talented people and heavily utilized many talents such as Ngo Thi Nham, Phan Huy Ich, Nguyen Thiep, Nguyen Huy Luong,... In terms of the military, he built an army equipped with modern weapons. Economically, he reformed the taxation and land systems, encouraged handicrafts, and expanded foreign trade with the West. Regarding education, he improved the examination system in a practical direction, promulgated policies to encourage learning, encouraged the use of pure Vietnamese Nom script instead of Han script, reorganized redundant temples, and eradicated superstitions. Historians highly appreciate these reforms because they had a very progressive tendency, surpassing contemporary Asian countries, and could bring the country out of the stagnation that had lasted over 100 years under the Trinh–Nguyen feudal system. Even much later (1822), overseas Chinese who lived in Hue under the Tay Son era still reminisced about Nguyen Hue's rule; they commented to a British merchant that Quang Trung ruled more mildly and fairly than the Nguyen dynasty kings (Gia Long and Minh Mang)[3] (see details at progressive reforms of King Quang Trung).

The active life of Nguyen Hue made a decisive contribution to the cause of national reunification of the Tay Son dynasty[4]. After 20 years of continuous warfare and 3 years of ruling the country, when the country's situation began to show positive changes, Quang Trung died suddenly at the age of 39. After his death, the Tay Son Dynasty weakened rapidly. His successor, Quang Toan, was still too young (9 years old) to lead Dai Viet; the royal court fell into internal conflict and failed to continue resisting Nguyen Anh.

Nguyen Hue is listed by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism as one of the 14 typical national heroes of Vietnam.[5] Many schools and streets in localities are named Quang Trung and Nguyen Hue.

Origins and Education

Origins of the Tay Son lineage

According to the books Khâm định Việt sử Thông giám cương mục and Đại Nam thực lục: Nguyen Hue's ancestors were from Hung Nguyen district, Nghe An region. Around the Thinh Duc years (reign name of Le Than Tong) (1653–1657), they were captured by Lord Nguyen's army and brought back to live in Tuy Vien district, Hoai Nhon prefecture. Several generations later, Nguyen Nhac held the position of a minor clerk (Biện lại) at the Van Don tollgate. The book Việt Nam sử lược adds: Nguyen Hue's father, Ho Phi Phuc, moved his home to Kien Thanh hamlet, and had 3 children: the eldest was Nhac, the second was Hue, and the third was Lu.[6][7] His mother was Nguyen Thi Dong.

According to the book Lịch sử nội chiến Việt Nam từ 1771 đến 1802 (History of the Vietnamese Civil War from 1771 to 1802), the ancestors of the Tay Son family originally bore the surname Ho in Huong Cai village, Hung Nguyen district, Nghe An province, descending from Ho Quy Ly,[8] and followed Lord Nguyen to settle in the South when Lord Nguyen crossed the Thay Wall to attack the Le–Trinh territory reaching Nghe An (in 1655). Nguyen Hue's great-grandfather, named Ho Phi Long (who worked for the Dinh family in Bang Chau village, Tuy Vien district, i.e., An Nhon), married a woman from the Dinh family and had a son named Ho Phi Tien. Ho Phi Tien did not pursue farming but went to sell betel in Tay Son hamlet, married, and settled there. Ho Phi Tien's wife was Nguyen Thi, the only daughter of a wealthy merchant in Phu Lac, hence they changed their children's surname from Ho to the mother's surname, Nguyen.[9] Their son, Nguyen Phi Phuc, also specialized in the betel trade and prospered. Some documents also suggest that the Ho family changed to the surname of Lord Nguyen as soon as they moved South.[8][9][10] Mr. Nguyen Phi Phuc had 8 children, including 3 sons: Nguyen Nhac, Nguyen Hue, and Nguyen Lu.

Nguyen Hue was born in the year of Quy Dau 1753, the 13th year of the Canh Hung reign under King Le Hien Tong of the Later Le Dynasty. He was also known as Quang Binh,[11] Van Hue[12], or Ho Thom. Later, local people often called him Duc Ong Binh (Lord Binh)[13] or Duc Ong Tam (Lord Eight).[8] According to Quang Trung anh hùng dân tộc (Quang Trung the National Hero), "Nguyen Hue had curly hair, textured skin, eyes that flashed like lightning, a voice as resonant as a bell, agile, strong, and brave." The book Tây Sơn lược also described Quang Trung's eyes: "at night, when sitting without a lamp, the light from his eyes illuminated the whole mat."

Regarding the birth order of Nguyen Hue and Nguyen Lu among the brothers, documentary sources are inconsistent: The books Đại Nam thực lục chính biên, Đại Nam chính biên liệt truyện, and Tây Sơn thủy mạt khảo all agree on the order of the three Tay Son brothers, affirming that: "The eldest is Nhac, next is Lu, and then Hue"; The people of old Quy Nhon prefecture passed down that: Nguyen Nhac traded betel in his youth, so he was called Anh Hai Trau (Brother Two Betel), Nguyen Hue was also named Thom, so he was called Chu Ba Thom (Uncle Three Thom), and Nguyen Lu was called Thay Tu Lu (Master Four Lu).[a] According to letters from Western missionaries active in Dai Viet at that time, such as Labartette, Eyet, and Varen, Nguyen Hue was Nguyen Lu's younger brother. Nguyen Lu was called Duc Ong Bay (Lord Seven) and Nguyen Hue was Duc Ong Tam (Lord Eight); Le Trong Ham in the book Minh đô sử argued that Ho Phi Phuc fathered "Nhac, Lu, then two daughters, and then Hue".

Growing up, he, Nguyen Nhac, and Nguyen Lu were sent to study both literature and martial arts with teacher Truong Van Hien. Truong Van Hien was a retainer of Truong Van Hanh, and Truong Van Hanh was the teacher of Nguyen Phuc Luan – Nguyen Anh's father. After Truong Van Hanh was harmed by the powerful courtier Truong Phuc Loan, Truong Van Hien fled to Binh Dinh. It was this teacher who discovered Nguyen Hue's abilities and advised the three brothers to rebel to build a great enterprise. Legend has it that the prophecy "Tay khoi nghia, Bac thu cong" (Rebel in the West, achieve success in the North) originated from Truong Van Hien.[14] Legend also has it that Nguyen Hue, Nguyen Nhac, and Nguyen Lu were all highly skilled in martial arts and were the founders of several Binh Dinh martial arts styles. Nguyen Hue created Yen Phi Quyen (Swallow Flight Form), Nguyen Lu created Hung Ke Quyen (Rooster Form), and all three Tay Son brothers created Doc Lu Thuong (Single Censer Spear). The three Tay Son heroes played a huge role in the formation and development of the Tay Son Binh Dinh martial arts sect, being the leaders who created and reformed empty-handed and weapon forms to teach the army in the early stages of the uprising.[15]
